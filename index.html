Pacific County, Washington · Local elections 2021, 2023 and 2025

Four out of five local seats had one name on the ballot.

Over the last three local election years, Pacific County voters were asked to fill 190 seats on city councils, school boards, fire districts, ports, hospital boards and other local bodies. In 153 of them, there was nothing to decide. Only one person ran.

Seats where nobody filed at all

Washington law gives an empty race a second chance. Here is what happens when filing week ends and a seat has no candidate.

  1. Filing week closes in May with nobody signed up. This also happens when the only person who filed withdraws.
  2. The county auditor must open filing again for three days. State law requires it. In 2025, Pacific County's second window ran May 28 to 30.
  3. The public hears about it through local news. There is no mailing to voters. Most people never know the window opened.
  4. Anyone who files in those three days goes straight to the November ballot. There is no primary. These are the medium blue ovals above. Across the three election years, 21 seats reached the ballot this way, and 20 of them ended up with one name.
  5. If still nobody files, the race is cancelled. The seat comes off the ballot, and write-in votes for it are not counted.

Money is rarely what stops people. Of the seven seats in the 2025 second window, only one had a filing fee, and it was $36.

2025: seven seats

  • Raymond City Council, Seat 1
  • North River School Board, Seats 2 and 4
  • Fire District 4 Commission, one seat
  • Chinook Water District, Seats 2 and 3
  • Sewer District 1, one seat

All seven drew one candidate in the second window, and all seven ran alone in November. Two of the three seats on the Chinook Water board were on this list at once.

Four more seats in districts shared with a neighboring county also drew nobody in filing week: Pe Ell School Board District 3, South Beach Regional Fire Authority Districts 2 and 3, and Park District 1 Position 1.

  • Naselle-Grays River Valley School Board, District 1

Four drew one candidate in the second window. Fire District 7 drew two. After filing week that year, 9 of the 60 open positions in the county had no candidate.

Three more in shared districts also drew nobody in filing week: Fire District 15 Positions 2 and 3, and South Beach Regional Fire Authority District 1. Two other seats were due in 2023 and do not appear on that year's ballot at all: Chinook Water District Position 2 and Fire District 15 Position 1. Both came back in 2025.

2021: two seats, and two cancelled races

  • Port of Ilwaco Commission, District 2

Each drew one candidate in the second window and ran alone.

Two fire commission seats went further. Fire District 2 Position 1 and Fire District 8 Position 1 were due for election in 2021, and neither appeared on the ballot. Both came back in 2023 with four years left on a six-year term. Each drew one candidate.

How the seat gets filled anyway

A cancelled race does not leave the seat empty. Someone still holds it and still votes on budgets, rates and policy. The difference is that voters did not choose that person.

If someone is already in the seat, they stay

State law keeps the current office holder in place until a successor is elected the next time that seat is up. For a city council seat with a four-year term, that can mean four more years without appearing on a ballot.

If the seat is empty, the council appoints

This happens when the office holder resigns, moves away or dies. In most cities and towns, the remaining council members pick a replacement by majority vote in an open meeting.

The appointment runs on a clock:

  1. The council has 90 days to appoint someone.
  2. If it does not, the county commissioners get the next 90 days.
  3. After 180 days, either one can ask the governor to make the appointment.

The person appointed serves until the next general election when that seat would normally be on the ballot, usually the next odd-numbered year. A mayor's seat is filled the same way. Fire, water, hospital and most other district boards follow a similar process, with the remaining board members making the choice.

In both cases, the decision rests with the few people already in office.

This is a recruitment problem, and it can be fixed

People do not run because nobody asks them, nobody explains what the job is, and filing week passes before they hear about it. Many of these seats have no filing fee. Many are won with a few hundred votes, and some fire commission seats with fewer than fifty.

A candidate recruitment program does three plain things: finds people who care about their fire district, school or water system, shows them what the seat involves, and walks them through filing. The next filing week for these local offices is the first week of May 2027.

How this was counted. Every candidate race on the Pacific County general election ballot in November 2021, 2023 and 2025, from certified results published by the Washington Secretary of State. A seat counts as having one name when a single candidate was printed on the ballot. Write-in lines are not counted as a second candidate. Ballot measures and one regional Court of Appeals seat are left out.

Shared districts. Fifty of the 190 seats belong to districts that cross into a neighboring county, such as Ocosta, North River, Pe Ell and Naselle-Grays River Valley schools. Counting only districts fully inside Pacific County, the result is 109 of 140 seats with one name, or 78 percent.

How the medium blue seats were found. The Washington Secretary of State publishes every candidate who filed during the regular filing week, with the date. A seat that is on the November ballot but has no one on that list drew no candidate in filing week. For Pacific County's own districts this matches the count of offices in each year's special filing period reported in the local press. The 2025 newspaper list calls the sewer seat Seat 1, and the ballot calls it Position 3.

What is not counted. The 190 seats are the ones that reached the ballot. The four races described above as cancelled or missing from the ballot are not in that number. They were identified from the term lengths in the state filing list and from which positions appeared in which year, so treat them as a careful reading of the record and not an official list. Seats filled by appointment between elections are also not counted. Fourteen seats came back to the ballot two years after their last election, which usually means someone left early and a replacement was appointed.
